# OW-8 — the duel dose-response: a cliff, and a knob that stops working

Date: 2026-08-23. Driver `ow8.py` (+ high-dose supplement); raw
data `ow8_dose.csv`; 1,240 runs, exact accounting on all. Verdict
up front: **the free-matter law war sits on a cliff — a 25% mover
boost flips a 2:1 favorite into a 6:1 underdog (crossing at
w* ≈ 1.08) — and pricing removes the cliff entirely: the same knob
turned 16× barely moves the priced duel (0.983 → 0.750). The
economy does not just shift the balance; it changes WHICH knob
governs the war.**

## Readings

1. **The free war is threshold-like** (pre-registered "sigmoid-
   steep" confirmed, sharper than predicted): the share falls
   0.660 → 0.150 across w ∈ [1, 1.25], w* ≈ 1.08, and is
   effectively decided (0.010) by w=1.5. Mechanism consistent with
   contested-rate amplification: painting buys territory, territory
   buys applicable mass, mass buys painting — a positive feedback
   that compounds any small rate edge into near-certain victory.
   Night-4's "selection acts on contested rates only" acquires its
   quantitative form: near parity, the contested rate has a
   derivative of order 2 share-units per weight-unit.
2. **Pricing makes the same knob impotent** (pre-registered as
   "crosses later"; reality is stronger). Slope near w=1: ~2.0 per
   unit w free vs ~0.017 priced — two orders of magnitude. Even
   w=16 leaves α at 0.750; extrapolation puts any crossing beyond
   w ≈ 100. Cause: weight buys win-probability *among applicable
   options*, and the priced world's binding constraint — a token
   under β's head — is not in the applicable set's control. No
   weight multiplies fuel. The binding constraint moved from
   event-share (weight-buyable) to catchment (weight-blind).
3. Joint with OW-7: the open world now has two demonstrated
   regime-dependent governance results — *who* wins changes with
   the economy (mix inversion), and *what matters* changes with
   the economy (rate-knob impotence).
