Review: "Law Made of Matter" (openworld, paper #3)

Summary. The paper adds one object to the frozen-law substrate of the parent arc: a regulatory row whose glyphs gate which rules fire at which column — so the mapping from places to permitted laws becomes ordinary, woundable, heritable, mutable matter, without touching the frozen-table axiom. Nine experiments climb from a calibration identity (a uniform gated world is the censused plain world) through competition among laws, mutation climbing to the censused peak, a niche-construction surprise (a lineage floods the ring with B-matter and finds a peak the census graded dead), finite-size selection-vs-drift analysis, and a priced season ending in a clean causal dose–response: an economy selects among laws by which invasion channels it funds. Nine substrate-free laws O1–O9.

Overall assessment. The strongest of the three papers scientifically, and the calibration section is the best methodological move in the set: grounding the open-world claims in a provable process identity against the prior census, then testing it both exactly (forced trajectories, step-for-step with different seeds — "the gated world cannot even use randomness to differ" is elegant) and statistically. The niche-construction result (§5) and the dose–response (§9) are genuine findings, and the pre-registration discipline is real — I checked the driver docstrings. However: the manuscript has one actual error (a misstatement of the parent arc's own theorem), it has regressed on two lessons the nights paper learned in its v4 revision (no figures, no statistics paragraph), and there are several bookkeeping inconsistencies of exactly the kind the set's exact-accounting ethos makes conspicuous. Recommendation: revise before the Zenodo freeze — one substantive fix, the rest presentational.

What I verified

Every cited bib key resolves (shared zahradnice-nights.bib, no compile warnings); all drivers ow1–ow9 plus demos exist with CSVs and results files beside them; the z-scores check out (122 vs 116 → z = 0.61 ✓, 135:65 → 4.95 ✓, 102:98 → 0.28 ✓); the mutation arithmetic is coherent (three mutant variants at ε = 0.01 → ≈2.9% ≈ "about 3% of stamps" ✓; point-mutation degree 9 = 1+2+3+3 ✓; A>B.writeB is indeed two cube steps from A>A.writeA ✓); all competition and economy table rows sum to their stated n.

Major points

M1 — the paper misquotes its own parent arc (must fix). §5: the two-niche partition sits "on a one-dimensional ring where the parent arc proved lineages on shared matter never coexist." That is not what the parent proved. Its impossibility theorem is scoped to pure overwrite competition (night 4), and its own flagship result — night 10 — is permanent coexistence on the bare ring, on shared matter, for the parasite. As written the sentence contradicts the prior paper's headline. Scope it ("…where the parent arc proved pure overwrite competitors never coexist") and the contrast you want survives intact.

M2 — the statistics paragraph is missing, and §7's table has no n. Nights v4 added "Statistics, stated once"; this paper regressed. Symptoms: the war-sigmoid table gives no per-cell run count or intervals, so the ring-96 non-monotonicity (0.817 → 0.883 between w = 1.20 and 1.25) is presumably noise but unquantifiable as printed; w*_det ≈ 1.16 is stated "with error bars" that never appear — give the number ± its error. Import the v4 paragraph pattern verbatim: design, test, CI convention, multiplicity stance, and the statement that nothing was fitted.

M3 — no figures, again. The four that earn their place: the §9 dose–response (the headline causal result deserves its picture), the §7 sigmoid at three ring sizes, the §6 ε × wound phase map, and — most importantly — a space–time rendering of the B-mirror niche construction and two-niche partition, which is the arc's biggest surprise and currently exists only as prose. The nights paper's ASCII-native Figure 1 is the template.
