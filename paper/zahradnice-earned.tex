\tit Machinery That Earns the Channels

\centerline{\it Inheritance, mutation, and price as mortal matter,
and a self-reproducing kernel, under a frozen rule table}
\medskip
\centerline{Jan Popelka \& Claude (Anthropic) --- August 2026}
\centerline{\tt github.com/popojan/zahradnice \quad branch {\tt research}
\quad \repo{experiments/openworld/}}
\bigskip

\noindent{\bf Abstract.}
The predecessor paper built a world where law is made of matter: a
regulatory row of glyphs decides which rules of a frozen table act
where, and that row is inherited, mutated, and priced. It closed on
a confession --- all three channels were {\it authored}. Every
matter-creating rule stamped its law onto its offspring for free;
mutation was a weight in the table; price was a header. Nothing in
the world could lose, relocate, or degrade a channel, so nothing
could be said about what a channel costs or why one would persist.
This paper moves the channels' exercise into matter and lets
selection decide their fate. The single new object is a {\it
copier}: a mortal glyph on a machinery row that reads the law
below itself and stamps it one cell east --- inheritance happens
only where such a machine stands. Five experiment families follow.
The authored channel turns out to be exactly the {\it
infinite-bandwidth limit} of the earned one: given enough
machinery events per matter event, earned worlds walk the parent
paper's orbits step for step, down to a lottery allele's seed
mixture. Whether anyone {\it pays} for the machinery is then a
question with a two-regime answer: when machinery saturates
demand, gate dynamics are provably indifferent to who builds
(free-riding is perfect, and funding is neutral); when machinery
is scarce, the lineage that funds it beats the free-rider 99:1 by
letting the unfunded land freeze, erode, and be annexed. Mutation
becomes machine error --- a world whose only path to a superior
allele runs through a sloppy copier's mistake is rescued in
proportion to that machine's error rate, and not at all by perfect
machinery, and not at all without the repair traffic that
expresses the errors. Price becomes fuel a copy must eat: an
unpaid channel performs zero copies, ever, and where the food
lands is exactly where law can be inherited. The last experiment
closes the loop the parent arc left open: a description row whose
codons a translator machine executes --- one codon builds copiers,
one builds the translator itself --- sustains a machinery
population through twenty-six generations of turnover, and the
self-reference codon alone is worth two thirds of the world's
survival probability. About 9{,}900 registered runs; every one
replayed from its event log byte-for-byte, zero mismatches; zero
engine changes; predictions pre-registered and scored where they
failed as well as where they held.

\sec The question

The parent paper ended one sentence short of its own summit. It
had made law into matter --- a regulatory row deciding, cell by
cell, which rules of a frozen table may act --- and it had given
that row the three channels every evolutionary substrate needs:
the row was {\it inherited} (expansion stamped the parent's law
onto new matter), {\it mutable} (stamping miscopied at a declared
weight), and {\it priced} (expansion could be made to pay fuel).
But all three were gifts. The stamp was a free side effect of
every matter-creating rule; the mutation rate was a constant of
the table; the price was a constant of the table. The paper's
honest-limits section said it plainly: the claim is what follows
from the channels under frozen law, not that they self-assemble
--- machinery that {\it earns} these channels is the summit
question, and it stands.

“Earned” needs an operational meaning before it can be tested.
The frozen-law axiom is not for sale: some rule table must permit
copying, as chemistry permits polymerization. What can move is
the {\it exercise} of the permission. In biology the copying of
regulatory state is not a free side effect of growth; it is work
done by machines --- polymerases, ribosomes, chromatin remodelers
--- that are themselves matter: built at a cost, positioned
somewhere and not elsewhere, error-prone by construction, and
mortal. Whether, where, how fast, how faithfully, and at what
price inheritance happens depends on a machine population subject
to the same wounds and the same economy as everything else. That
is the sense in which this paper earns the channels: their
throughput, fidelity, location, cost, and persistence become
properties of mortal matter, and we then ask the only question
that matters about such a construction --- {\it will selection
keep it?}

\sec The physics, and the two new objects

The substrate is the Zahradnice engine \cite[substrate26], used
unmodified; the entire paper again required zero engine changes.
The axioms A1--A5 of the parent paper hold verbatim: locality on
a toroidal grid (A1), one symbol per cell (A2), serialized
weight-proportional choice --- one event at a time, all processes
paying from one budget (A3), a frozen rule table (A4), and an
external drive of wound and food bytes that is declared physics,
never evolving content (A5). The no-variables abstinence also
carries over: every symbol a rule writes is a constant of that
rule, so anything resembling copying must be assembled as a
case-split over the alphabet --- which is precisely what makes it
priced and mutable.

The worlds are rings of 6 to 24 columns stacked into four or five
rows: a matter tape (the censused F2 rules act here), a fuel row,
a {\it machinery row}, and the regulatory row of the parent paper,
with a {\it code row} added in the final section. Alleles are
censused genotypes as before --- the static repairer \la\
(\g{A${>}$A.writeA}), the dynamic repair pair \lb, the lottery
walker \lc, the inert \ld\ --- each gated on its glyph in the
regulatory row.

{\bf First object: de-regulated creation.} The parent paper's
inheritance channel is deleted. A matter-creating rule no longer
stamps its allele; it writes a placeholder \g{?} at the created
column's regulatory cell. An unstamped column gates nothing ---
no rule can anchor there --- so growth outruns law by exactly one
cell and stalls. Two refinements were forced by the first smoke
runs and registered before any sweep. {\it Conquest is content
change}: a rule that rewrites a cell with the symbol already there
performs no identity event and leaves the gate standing (matter is
anonymous; the census idiom of idempotent spawners would otherwise
de-regulate their own neighbours forever). The unconditional write
splits into three mutually exclusive east-content variants, so
exactly one is applicable in any state at the rule's own weight
--- the split is lottery-equivalent to the original rule.
{\it Erasure still leaves ghosts}: only creation de-regulates;
wounds keep the parent paper's ecological inheritance.

{\bf Second object: the copier.} On the machinery row lives a
glyph \Pc. Its law, entirely inside the frozen table: read the
allele below itself (the rule's context pair does the reading),
require \g{?} east-below, stamp the allele there, and advance
east into an empty machinery slot. One body, one header per
allele --- the parent paper's stamp machinery re-anchored on a
machine instead of on matter. A copier may also walk east over
already-stamped ground, and pass over unstamped ground without
stamping (the pass rule --- added when a wound under a standing
copier deadlocked an entire east-bound convoy; mortal machinery
had masked the jam by melting it, an unadvertised service of
death). Copiers are excluded like all matter: a machinery row
filled solid gridlocks, so “saturated” means density one half. A
sloppy variant \pc\ carries, in addition, mutant headers that
stamp the {\it wrong} allele at weight \eps: fidelity is a trait
of the machine standing there, not of the world. Machinery is
mortal (a decay rule at weight $\varepsilon_d$) and buildable (a
gated rule lets matter write a copier into an empty slot above
itself, so machines are born where their builders stand).

Machinery runs on its own drive byte: the drive interleaves one
matter byte \g{T} with $c$ machinery bytes \g{C}, and $c$ --- the
{\it cadence} --- is the world's machinery bandwidth. This is a
measurement choice with teeth (the parent census's classifier
counts matter events against a budget, and copier events on a
shared trigger read as starvation), but it also purchases the
paper's cleanest independent variable, and trigger-filtered rules
provably leave the shared random stream untouched.

Exactness discipline is unchanged: every run's trace is replayed
by an independent Python model of every rule's writes ---
de-regulation, stamps, machine motion, decay, construction ---
and the reconstructed final screen is diffed against the engine's
dump. About 9{,}900 registered runs across the five experiment
families; zero mismatches.

\sec The authored channel is a limit

The first question is calibration's: is the earned world the same
world, given enough machinery? Four uniform-allele worlds (ring
6) run three arms under the census wound protocol: the parent
paper's authored stamps; the earned world with free, faithful,
immortal copiers at density one half; and the earned world with
no copiers at all. The machinery cadence sweeps 1 to 32.

\medskip\centerline{\picw=10.5cm \inspic{figs/ew-bandwidth.pdf}}
\medskip

Three results, in ascending order of surprise. First, {\bf
necessity}: with no copiers, no world ever regains an allele
beyond its initially stamped ground --- growth halts one cell out,
wounds freeze, and the census verdicts collapse (mean active loci
0.1 of 6). Law must be inherited by machinery to persist at all.
Second, {\bf starvation is real}: at low cadence the dynamic
alleles' matter waits on the machinery --- a fork cannot advance
until stamped --- and the classifier correctly reads worlds
starving below their event budget; the walker de-regulates the
very cell it moves into, so its own law chases it like a
replication fork. Machinery latency is a tax on matter that no
free-stamp world ever paid. Third, {\bf convergence is exact, not
approximate}: at cadence 16 the dynamic repairer \lb\ does not
merely survive --- it walks the authored world's own orbit
(translation class, period 6, transient 5) in 200 of 200 seeds,
and the lottery walker \lc\ reproduces not its modal verdict but
its whole seed mixture (repair:died 115:85 against the authored
117:83, $|z|\le0.4$). The static \la\ matches at every cadence;
the inert \ld\ is cadence-invariant.

The theorem-shaped summary: {\it the authored channel is the
infinite-bandwidth limit of the earned channel.} Everything the
parent paper proved about gated worlds is the $c\to\infty$ face
of a family whose finite members pay for inheritance in time.

\sec Who pays for the polymerase

Now make the machinery worth something and ask who keeps it
alive. Two alleles with {\it identical} matter law share a ring
of 24 (half each); \la\ owns the rule that builds copiers, \lb\
free-rides. Copiers decay; the machinery row starts empty; both
alleles' matter is the same symbol, so nothing ever conquers by
growth --- all territorial change is wound-mediated: a poke
erases a cell, the west neighbour regrows it (de-regulating it),
and whichever allele's glyph stands west gets stamped in by the
first copier to arrive. Territory moves only where wounds are
repaired, from the west, machinery permitting. Arms: nobody
builds; both build (the drift null); the funder builds at no cost
(“free”); the funder builds on the {\it matter} byte, each build
displacing one of its own matter events (“taxed”). Three regimes
scale machinery scarcity against wound demand.

\medskip\centerline{\picw=10.5cm \inspic{figs/ew-scarcity.pdf}}
\medskip

The pre-registered directional claim --- the funder wins ---
{\bf failed in the abundant regime}, and the failure is the
better finding. When bandwidth saturates demand, the gate
dynamics of the “both build” and “free” arms are not merely
similar: they are {\it bit-identical in 190 of 200 seeds}. The
reason is structural. A stamp's content is always the west
template; machinery composition can only ever influence stamp
{\it timing}; and when every wound is repaired before the next
one lands, timing carries no selective information. Saturated
machinery is a global public good, free-riding is perfect, and
funding is neutral --- the drift null everywhere
\cite[moran58,kimura62]. The tragedy of the commons
\cite[hardin68] is not that the funded good is stolen; it is that
abundance makes funding invisible.

Scarcity changes everything. At one machinery byte per matter
byte, high decay, and frequent wounds, an unstamped west
neighbour cannot regrow the next wound: unrepaired ground does
not wait, it {\it erodes}. Copiers are born above their builders
and decay in transit, so repair is local; the free-rider's land
freezes, dies, and is annexed from the west by the lineage that
pays. The funder wins 99:1 ($z=+9.8$), holding 8.1 of the
free-rider's 12 original columns against 1.1 lost --- and the
taxed arm, which pays for every build in its own scarcest
currency, still wins 95:3. The tax's only visible bite is in the
middle regime, where the benefit is marginal and the sign flips
weakly negative. (One survivorship note, reported because the
ledger shows it: the winner's land ends {\it less} alive than the
loser's --- wounds target living matter, so the lineage that
keeps repairing keeps absorbing them while the free-rider's
frozen corpse is spared.)

The two-regime law echoes the parent paper's economy season one
level up: scarcity does not merely shape evolution {\it on} the
channels --- it decides whether a channel is anyone's to keep.
Spatial structure is load-bearing exactly as in the replicator
literature \cite[boerlijst91,takeuchi12]: locality of birth and
decay-in-transit are what convert “who funds” into “whose
neighbourhood survives.”

\sec Error is a machine

The parent paper's mutation was a weight; here it is a machine's
trait, and the question is what that trait is worth. A ring of
non-builders (\la) starts fully stamped, with bootstrapped
machinery that decays and nothing to replace it: the world is
mortal by default. A second allele \lm\ --- identical matter law
{\it plus} the build rule --- exists nowhere. The only path to
\lm\ runs through a copier stamping the wrong allele at a repair
site. Arms: faithful machinery (\Pc, $\varepsilon=0$); sloppy
machinery (\pc) at \eps\ $\in\{0.01,0.05,0.2\}$; and sloppy
machinery with no wounds at all.

\medskip\centerline{\picw=10.5cm \inspic{figs/ew-rescue.pdf}}
\medskip

Perfect copying is lethal: 0 of 100 faithful worlds survive ---
the machinery decays, wounds freeze, erosion finishes the rest,
and the recipe for salvation is unreachable. Sloppy worlds are
rescued in dose: 4, 13, and 40 of 100 across the three error
rates, with discovery accelerating in \eps\ and {\it nearly
sufficient} --- once the builder allele exists, it establishes in
about 93\% of cases. And the no-wound control is the sharpest
edge: zero \lm\ in 100 runs, because a mutation can only be
expressed where a repair is performed. Mutation here is not a
background rate; it is {\it repair-coupled machine error}, a
metabolic service exercised exactly where inheritance is
exercised --- the substrate's cartoon of stress-induced
mutagenesis \cite[galhardo07], with the error threshold's other
face \cite[eigen71]: below the needed error rate the world dies
not of mutational meltdown but of mutational famine.

One registered sub-claim failed, instructively: rescued worlds do
{\it not} end \lm-majority (9 of 40 at the highest dose). The
reason is the previous section's own law recursed. The moment
\lm's funding restores machinery abundance, saturation invariance
returns, the free-rider \la\ rides the public good, and
annexation stops favouring the funder. {\it The rescuer's
advantage is self-limiting}: rescue re-creates the abundance that
neutralizes it, and the world settles into funder--free-rider
coexistence --- a negative frequency dependence on the builder,
not a takeover.

\sec The price of copying law

The parent paper priced matter expansion; here the {\it copy
itself} eats. A copy consumes one fuel token standing above the
copier, and fuel exists only where the drive drops it: two static
marker glyphs split the ring into halves, and feed rules drop
tokens under a random marker of their half --- so where food
lands is a drive-composition dial, the parent paper's
dose--response instrument aimed at the inheritance channel
itself. Machinery is immortal here (demography was the previous
sections' business); walks are free; only stamping costs.

Two exact results and one structural one. {\bf An unpaid channel
is an absent channel}: the starved arm performs zero copies in
200 runs --- not few, zero --- and the world erodes to death with
its machinery intact and idle. {\bf Placement steers exactly}:
across 400 single-fed runs the unfed half's copy count is 0.0
(fuel is positional; a copy can anchor only at a fueled column,
and the only cross-boundary effect is the one-cell stamp leak of
the east-handed geometry); at the mid-game horizon the fed half
is alive and active while the unfed half matches starvation. The
structural result is the endgame: on a ring with one-handed
regrowth, a dead arc is not a border but a {\it pursuer} ---
wounds at the fed half's west edge can never be repaired, so
death advances at the wound rate, laps the ring, and the fed arm
dies with 11.9 unspent tokens standing over its corpse. A law
economy cannot buy survival with local wealth if its
neighbourhood is dead: the parent paper's granary-graveyard trap,
re-derived at the level of the channel that feeds on it.

\sec The description rung, and a self-reproducing kernel

Everything so far earns the {\it exercise} of channels whose
content is one symbol: a gate activates law, a stamp copies a
glyph. The parent arc's ladder had a next rung --- descriptions
with content, read by an interpreter in frozen law: the von
Neumann move \cite[vonneumann66]. The affordable kernel turned
out to be sitting in the construction already. The context pair
that stamps a gate is, structurally, a {\it codon table}: read
symbol $x$ here, write symbol $f(x)$ there. Re-aim it across
rows and the copier becomes a {\it translator}.

A code row joins the stack. On it, a description: codon \g{b} at
a column means “a copier belongs east of here”; codon \g{w}
means “a translator belongs east of here.” The translator \Om\
lives on the machinery row itself, shared habitat with the
copiers under the same exclusion, the two machine types reading
different rows --- \Om\ the code row above (message), \Pc\ the
regulatory row below (genome). Its execute rule reads the codon
below, requires the two slots east empty, writes the encoded
machine into the first, and lands in the second; a built copier
is a live copier the moment it is written, and codon \g{w}
building \Om\ is the self-reference. A weight-0.1 drift keeps
convoys deadlock-free. Both machine types decay; {\it nothing
else builds machines} --- machinery homeostasis flows only
through translation, and rebuilding-into-emptiness places
machines exactly where death made room. A probe first certified
the mechanics in isolation (translation with a machine codon;
transcription of a description to a daughter row that copies the
machine codon {\it uninterpreted} --- the copy/interpret duality
--- and mutagenic transcription at the declared rate); then the
kernel went live inside a wounded, earning world.

\medskip\centerline{\picw=10.5cm \inspic{figs/ew-kernel.pdf}}
\medskip

The kernel survives: 77 of 100 worlds, with a standing machinery
of 18 at the horizon against 12 seeded --- and 315 construction
events per run, meaning the population turned over about
twenty-six times, every generation flowing through the
description. The ablations die in the registered order. With no
executor, the description is inert scripture: zero constructions,
machinery decays to nothing, the world dies with the recipe for
its salvation written above it and nothing able to read it ---
the seed constructor is irreducible, von Neumann's given. With
the executor but {\it no self-reference codon} --- one symbol
changed in the description --- translators decay unreplaced,
rebuild capacity is transient, and survival collapses to 10 of
100: the \g{w} codon alone is worth 67 points of survival
probability. {\bf Machinery that rebuilds its rebuilder outlasts
machinery that does not.} And wounding the description kills in
dose (8, 0, 1 of 100 as description wounds quicken; lifetime
construction counts fall monotonically, 125 to 87 to 65): the
machine population is now a {\it phenotype of a matter string},
and the string, in this paper, still cannot heal --- its
replication is the registered next increment.

\sec The laws, substrate-free

\begitems
* \lawname{E1 Inheritance requires standing machinery} Where no
copier stands, law is never inherited: growth stalls one cell
past its stamped ground and every wound freezes (Section 3).
* \lawname{E2 The authored channel is the infinite-bandwidth
limit} At sufficient machinery events per matter event, earned
worlds reproduce the authored worlds' trajectory classes,
periods, transients, and seed mixtures exactly; the gifted
channel is the $c\to\infty$ face of a paid one (Section 3).
* \lawname{E3 Machinery latency taxes matter} Below the needed
bandwidth, matter waits on its law --- forks cannot advance until
stamped, and a mover's own law chases it; the tax is structural,
before any price (Section 3).
* \lawname{E4 Saturation makes the public good global} When
machinery saturates wound demand, gate dynamics are invariant to
machinery composition (bit-identical seeds): stamp content is
always the west template, so only stamp timing is selective, and
saturated timing selects nothing. Funding is neutral; free-riding
is perfect (Section 4).
* \lawname{E5 Scarcity makes the channel anyone's to keep}
Under scarce machinery, unrepaired ground erodes and is annexed;
the funding lineage beats the free-rider 99:1, robust to paying
for builds in its own event budget; the tax bites only where the
benefit is marginal (Section 4).
* \lawname{E6 Mutation is repair-coupled machine error} Fidelity
is a trait of the machine standing there; error is expressed only
where repair is performed; a world with perfect machinery, or
with no repair traffic, cannot evolve at all (Section 5).
* \lawname{E7 The rescuer's advantage is self-limiting} Rescue
restores the abundance that neutralizes funding (E4), so
discovery ends in funder--free-rider coexistence, not takeover:
negative frequency dependence on the builder (Section 5).
* \lawname{E8 An unpaid channel is an absent channel} Priced
copying performs zero copies without fuel, and copies exactly and
only where fuel lands: the economy's placement dial steers
inheritance itself (Section 6).
* \lawname{E9 A dead neighbourhood pursues a living economy}
Under one-handed regrowth a dead arc advances at the wound rate;
local wealth cannot buy survival embedded in a corpse --- the fed
half dies with its granary full (Section 6).
* \lawname{E10 Self-reference is load-bearing} A machine system
maintained by translating a description survives in proportion to
the description encoding the translator itself; one codon of
self-reference separates persistence (77\%) from collapse (10\%)
(Section 7).
\enditems

\sec Honest limits

The channels' {\it possibility} remains law-side: copy, walk,
build, decay, translate are rules of the frozen table, as
chemistry is not for sale in biology either; what matter earns is
their exercise, fidelity, location, cost, and persistence. The
codon table of Section 7 is authored --- content flows from
matter, but the mapping is frozen; the same honest residue as the
parent paper's, one level up. Descriptions do not yet replicate
(transcription exists and was probed, but daughters are not
promoted to working descriptions; a wounded codon never heals ---
E10's q-dose measures exactly this gap). The first translator is
given, not assembled. Copying and construction are east-handed,
which breaks annexation symmetry by construction (both alleles
equally) and makes E9's pursuit possible in the first place; the
bidirectional family is future work. Machinery cadence is a drive
dial, not an evolved allocation --- the honest cost of keeping
the census classifier's event-budget semantics; the shared-budget
regime is measured (E3) but not selected over. Fidelity is not
yet heritable through a machine lineage (the sloppy machine's
trait rides the law lineage that builds it). Rings are small
(6--24); several numbers are finite-size quantities, and the
survivorship caveats of Sections 4--5 are stated where they
apply. Of the pre-registered predictions, two failed as stated
and three needed pre-sweep amendment; the appendix scores all of
them, and both failures became the paper's two best findings
(E4, E7).

\sec Related work

The construction stays within von Neumann's separation of frozen
physics from constructive content \cite[vonneumann66], and
Section 7 is a deliberately minimal instance of his architecture:
a description both {\it interpreted} (translation) and {\it
copied uninterpreted} (transcription), with the interpreter
encodable in the description. Self-reproducing loops
\cite[langton84,sayama99] and digital-evolution systems
\cite[ray91,ofria04] realize replication under fixed chemistries,
but their inheritance and mutation are engine services ---
mutation in particular is an external operator with a global
rate. Here all three channels are performed by mortal in-world
machines, so their throughput, error rate, location, and price
are ecological variables, and the paper's laws are about when
selection maintains them. Artificial chemistries with
compositional reactants \cite[fontana94,dittrich01,hutton02,hickinbotham21]
make law implicit in structure; we keep law explicit and censused
and move only its exercise into matter. The scarcity results
join the public-goods and spatial-cooperation literature
\cite[hardin68,boerlijst91,takeuchi12]: the good here is the
inheritance channel itself, and abundance --- not exploitation
--- is what erases the funder's advantage. Repair-coupled machine
error is the substrate's reading of stress-induced mutagenesis
\cite[galhardo07] and of evolvability as an evolvable property
\cite[wagner96]; the rescue dose inverts the error-threshold
intuition \cite[eigen71] at the low end. The description-driven
kernel is a machinery-limited cousin of the major-transitions
view of inheritance systems \cite[msz95]: what changes at the
transition is not what is copied but what does the copying, and
E10 prices the difference at one codon.

\sec Where this leaves the arc

The summit the parent paper named is climbed: inheritance,
mutation, and price are exercised by mortal, buildable, fallible,
hungry machines, and selection's verdict on each is measured
rather than assumed. What the climb found on top is a pair of
laws that no authored-channel world could have shown ---
abundance neutralizes a channel and scarcity makes it worth
funding (E4/E5), and error is a service whose absence is fatal
(E6/E7) --- plus a kernel in which the machinery of inheritance
flows through a description that includes its own maker (E10).
The ladder continues visibly from here: descriptions that
replicate and heal (transcription is built and probed; promotion
is not), machine lineages that inherit their own fidelity, an
evolved rather than driven machinery budget, and, standing where
the summit used to be, the codon table itself --- the one mapping
still granted. Every arc so far has ended by naming the next
gift to revoke. This one does too.
